The problem: a payment link that never arrived

Half of our new customers were getting an embarrassing experience, and store clerks stopped recommending us.

Before: Payment link

1

The customer selects Aplazo at the counter.

2

The clerk sends a payment link by SMS or WhatsApp.

3

The link opens checkout, where the customer picks an installment plan.

Business problems

Fewer in-store payments than expected. Only 25% of new customers activated (made a first purchase), and BNPL Offline GMV was 0.08B in January 2026.

Clerks stopped offering Aplazo. The hardest insight from our field research was knowing that stores perceived our payment method as painful and decided to stop promoting it.

The best possible experience for paying in stores with Aplazo.

What we found

The main issue was connectivity. Signal inside most stores was poor.

Customers

Embarrassed at the counter. They'd rather use another payment method or abandon the purchase.

Clerks

Stopped offering Aplazo. A slow checkout wasn't worth promoting.

Pain 01

The link arrived late, or never arrived at all.

Pain 02

About 2 minutes average per purchase, while the line grew.

Pain 03

Anxious customers who never wanted to repeat it, and switched payment methods.

In their words

“Even if I need the credit and I wanted to pay with it, it is very embarrassing that it never worked, I'll look for another option.”

Aplazo · In-store payments · 2026

Iteration 1

A QR code on the merchant's screen

Our first and fastest iteration. The customer opens the camera, scans, and goes to checkout.

Problem 01

Not every Android camera reads QR codes.

Many customers had to download a separate app first. So we had to build an In App QR reader feature.

Problem 02

The screen faced the wrong way.

Clerks turned the monitor around, or customers stepped into the cashier area to scan it.

Verdict

Better than the link, but not practical at a real counter.

Rafael Morales

Aplazo · In-store payments · 2026

Iteration 2

The Customer ID code

Most stores already had scanners for their products. So we built on top of them: a unique, temporary code for each customer, shown as a barcode and a QR code.

1

The customer chooses Aplazo and shows their Customer ID.

2

The clerk scans it with the scanner they already use (Barcode or QR).

3

The customer goes straight to checkout and finishes.

No reaching over the counter. No new hardware.
Coverage for a wide range of merchant types.

What's next · In progress

The prepaid loan

The last step to remove is checkout itself. Before reaching the counter, the customer picks the store and the amount. Then they tap or scan, and the payment is confirmed. Nothing else.

Valid for 24 hours.

Works with every method we built: QR code, Customer ID and NFC.

One biometric confirmation. When the merchant matches a prepaid loan, we take that amount directly.

More than a faster payment: setting a budget and sticking to it helps customers use their credit more responsibly.

Problems and goals

Clara won startups. To keep growing, it had to win enterprise finance teams.

User problems

Missing workflows. Enterprise finance teams needed approvals, card controls and supplier payments.

More of everything. More people, more controls, more entities, more systems to connect.

Business problems

Growth meant moving upmarket. Startups were won. Mid-market and enterprise were next.

Without losing the base. Small customers made Clara successful and had to stay happy.

Revenue context. Plenty of prospects were on hold due to missing enterprise grade features.

Goals

Win mid-market and enterprise

The constraint

Build what large teams need without degrading the experience for small customers.
